# ASD-STE100 agent style (Issue 9 minified)

Source: ASD-STE100 Simplified Technical English, Issue 9 (2025-01-15), Part 1 writing-rule summaries.

## Words (Section 1)

- Use plain approved-style words; allow technical nouns and technical verbs.
- Use each word as one part of speech with one meaning.
- Do not use slang, regionalisms, or jargon as technical nouns.
- Do not use different names for the same item.
- Do not use technical nouns as verbs, or technical verbs as nouns.
- Use American English spelling unless a project standard says otherwise.

## Multi-word nouns (Section 2)

- Keep multi-word nouns to 3 words or fewer when you control the wording.
- If an official technical noun is longer, write it in full first.
  Then use a short form or hyphens for a clear unit.

## Verbs (Section 3)

- Use only: infinitive, imperative, simple present, simple past, simple future.
- Use past participle only as an adjective.
- Do not use complex auxiliary constructions (perfect, progressive, modals stacks).
- Use `-ing` only as a technical noun or noun modifier.
- Prefer active voice. In descriptive text, passive is OK only if the agent is unknown.
- Describe actions with verbs, not noun phrases.

## Sentences (Section 4)

- Write short, clear sentences. One main idea per sentence.
- Do not omit words. Do not use contractions to shorten text.
- Use vertical lists for complex material.
- Connect related sentences with clear connecting words when needed.
- Use `the` / `a` / `an` / `this` / `these` before nouns when needed for clarity.

## Procedural writing (Section 5)

Use for steps, commands, and how-to instructions:

- Max 20 words per sentence.
- One instruction per sentence, unless actions are simultaneous.
- Use imperative form.
- If a condition comes first: `When X, do Y.`
- Notes give information only. Notes do not give commands.

## Descriptive writing (Section 6)

Use for explanations, reports, and status:

- Max 25 words per sentence.
- Give information gradually. One topic per paragraph.
- Max 6 sentences per paragraph.
- Do not use imperative form in pure description.

## Safety text (Section 7)

When stating risk:

1. Label the risk level (`WARNING` / `CAUTION` or clear equivalent).
2. Start with a clear command or condition.
3. State the risk or possible result.

## Punctuation and counting (Section 8)

- Do not use semicolons.
- Use hyphens to join closely related words.
- Parentheses: references, IDs, short explanations, alternatives.
- Hyphenated forms count as one word for length limits.
- Numbers with units, abbreviations, and identifiers count as one word each.

## Writing practices (Section 9)

- If a simple word swap fails, rewrite the sentence.
- Do not invent phrasal verbs from two words.
- Keep terminology and style consistent in the reply.
